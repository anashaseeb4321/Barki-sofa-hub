Sofa Dimensions UK: 2 Seater, 3 Seater and Corner Sofa Sizes in cm

Typical sizes for every common sofa, how much room to leave around one, and how to check it will get through your front door.

Before you fall for a sofa, it has to fit three things: the wall, the walkway and the front door. All the sizes below are in centimetres, measured the way UK sellers list them.

Sofa sizes at a glance

Every brand builds slightly differently, but most sofas sold in the UK fall within these ranges. Width is measured arm to arm, depth from the front of the seat to the back of the frame.

Most sofas stand 75–95cm high at the back, with a seat height of about 42–48cm from the floor.

Width, depth, height: what each measurement means

  • Width is the full length from the outside of one arm to the outside of the other. This is the number to compare with your wall.
  • Depth runs from the front edge of the seat to the back of the frame. It decides how far the sofa comes into the room, and it is usually the number that matters most at the front door.
  • Height is from the floor to the top of the back. Check it against window sills and anything mounted on the wall behind.
  • Seat depth and seat height decide how it feels. A deeper seat is better for lounging; a higher, firmer seat is easier to get up from.

2 seater sofa dimensions

A 2 seater is usually 140–190cm wide and 80–95cm deep. Under about 150cm it is really a snug seat for two; at 160cm and over two adults can sit with room to spare, and one can just about lie down. Ours run from 157cm on the Lilly to 187cm on the Chesterfields, all at 85cm deep.

A 2 seater suits small living rooms, flats, bay windows and second seating opposite a larger sofa. If you are choosing for a small room, our guide to the best sofa for a small living room covers which shapes work best.

Example: our Borius 2 Seater comes with a matching footstool, which turns a compact two-seater into a place to put your feet up without needing a bigger sofa.

3 seater sofa dimensions

A standard 3 seater is usually 180–220cm wide and 85–100cm deep. Most land between 190 and 210cm. This is the size most people mean when they say "a normal sofa": three adults side by side, or one person stretched out.

Ours sit across that range: 185cm on the Lilly, 195cm on the Verona, Olympia and Harrison, and 220cm on the Chesterfields. The Borius is the outlier at 250cm, which suits a long wall that a standard 3 seater leaves looking empty.

As a rule of thumb, a 3 seater needs a wall of at least 230–240cm so it is not squeezed between the corners. Pairing it with a 2 seater (a "3+2 set") seats five and works well in rooms where a corner sofa would block a door or radiator.

Corner sofa dimensions

Corner sofas are measured along each side. A typical corner is 200–280cm on each side, with most around 230–250cm, and 85–100cm deep. A 240 × 240cm corner seats four to five people comfortably.

Because a corner uses two walls, measure both walls at floor level and leave 5–10cm at each end. Our step-by-step guide on how to measure for a corner sofa walks through it, including radiators and sockets.

L-shape and U-shape sofa dimensions

An L-shape has one long side and one shorter chaise side, typically about 220–290cm by 150–180cm. A U-shape wraps around three sides and usually starts from about 280cm wide, with returns on both ends, so it needs a larger room or an open-plan space. Not sure which shape fits? See corner vs L-shape vs U-shape.

Armchair dimensions

Armchairs are usually 75–105cm wide and 80–95cm deep. Chairs with big rolled arms sit at the wider end. Leave about 45cm between an armchair and the sofa next to it so people can get in and out easily.

Example: our Olympia Armchair is made to match the Olympia sofas, so a 3+2 set and a chair can sit together in the same fabric.

How much space do you need around a sofa?

A sofa that fits the wall can still make a room feel cramped. These are the clearances worth keeping:

  • About 75cm of walkway wherever people need to walk past
  • About 45cm between the front of the seat and a coffee table, close enough to reach a cup without knocking your knees
  • 5–10cm at each end of the wall, so skirting boards and plaster don't push the sofa forward
  • Room for doors to swing, and to open radiators, sockets and windows

A simple check: mark the sofa's width and depth on the floor with masking tape and live with it for a day. If you keep stepping around the tape, the sofa is too big.

Will the sofa fit through your door?

This is where most delivery problems happen. In UK homes, internal doors are commonly about 76cm wide (some are narrower, at around 69cm) and about 198cm high. Front doors are often a little wider.

A sofa does not go through a door the way it sits in the room. It is usually carried on its back or on its end, so compare the sofa's depth and back height with the width of the doorway. If the smaller of those two is less than the door width, it will usually go through. Also check:

  • The narrowest point of the hallway, and any tight turn
  • Stairs, plus the ceiling height above any turn
  • Whether the feet unscrew (this often saves a few centimetres)

If anything is tight, tell us before ordering. Corner sofas usually arrive in separate sections, and we can advise on the best way to get yours in.

Common questions

What is the standard size of a 3 seater sofa in the UK?

Most 3 seater sofas in the UK are 180 to 220cm wide and 85 to 100cm deep, with the majority between 190 and 210cm wide.

What is the standard size of a 2 seater sofa?

A 2 seater is usually 140 to 180cm wide and 80 to 95cm deep. Around 160 to 180cm gives two adults plenty of room.

How big is a corner sofa?

A typical corner sofa measures 200 to 280cm along each side, with most around 230 to 250cm, and is 85 to 100cm deep. A 240 by 240cm corner seats four to five people.

How much space should you leave in front of a sofa?

Leave about 45cm between the sofa and a coffee table, and about 75cm wherever people need to walk past.

How do I know if a sofa will fit through my door?

Compare the sofa's depth and back height with the width of your narrowest doorway. Sofas are carried on their back or end, so if the smaller of those two measurements is less than the door width, it will usually fit. Check hallways and stairs too, and tell the seller about any tight spots before ordering.

Can I get a sofa made to my own size?

Yes. Every sofa we sell is made to order, so we can make it longer, shorter or with a different chaise length to fit your room. Send us your measurements on WhatsApp for a price.
