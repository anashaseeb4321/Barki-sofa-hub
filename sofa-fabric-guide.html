Sofa Fabric Guide: Which Upholstery Actually Lasts?

Fabric decides how a sofa feels, how it ages, and how much of your weekend you spend cleaning it. Here's an honest comparison of the six fabrics we upholster in.

The short version

FabricBest forWatch out for
Plush VelvetLuxury feel, rich colour depthShows pressure marks; needs occasional brushing
Crush VelvetSheen and drama; hides marks wellStrong look — not for every room
ChenilleEveryday family use, warmthCan attract pet hair
Jumbo CordTexture, casual rooms, durabilityRidges trap crumbs — vacuum along the grain
NapleNeutral, hard-wearing wovenLess plush underhand than velvet
KensingtonPremium weave, tight finishHigher price point

Plush velvet

The softest option and the one most people reach for when they want the sofa to be the focal point of the room. Colour reads deeper in velvet than any other fabric because the pile catches light differently depending on the angle.

The trade-off is pressure marking — sit in the same spot and the pile flattens. This isn't damage and brushes out, but if a permanently pristine look matters to you, a woven fabric will suit you better.

Crush velvet

Velvet with a deliberately irregular pile, giving a rippled sheen. Because the surface is already varied, it hides marks and everyday use far better than plain velvet — which makes it a surprisingly practical choice despite looking the most dramatic.

Chenille

Our most popular family fabric. Chenille is woven from yarn with short piles bound into it, which gives it a soft, slightly fuzzy hand and real warmth. It wears well, resists pilling, and is forgiving with daily use.

If you have long-haired pets, expect to vacuum more often — the texture holds hair more than a flat weave does.

Jumbo cord

Wide, chunky ribs with a casual, tactile look. Very hard-wearing and good in rooms that take a lot of traffic. The one maintenance note: crumbs settle into the channels, so vacuum along the grain rather than across it.

Naple and Kensington

Both are flat woven fabrics rather than piled. Naple is our workhorse neutral — tight, durable, and unfussy. Kensington is a step up in quality of weave, with a denser finish that holds its shape over years of use.

Neither will mark or flatten the way velvet does, which makes them the sensible pick for a sofa that has to look consistent in a busy household.

Cleaning any of them

  • Blot spills immediately — never rub, which pushes liquid into the foam
  • Vacuum with a brush attachment weekly; it removes grit that would otherwise cut the fibres
  • Test any cleaning product on a hidden section first
  • Rotate and plump cushions weekly so they wear evenly
  • Keep the sofa out of direct sunlight where possible — UV fades every fabric eventually

Which should you choose?

  • Young children: Chenille or Naple — forgiving and easy to clean
  • Pets: Naple or Kensington — flat weaves release hair more easily
  • Statement room: Plush or Crush Velvet
  • Heavy daily use: Jumbo Cord or Kensington
  • Rental or short-term: Naple — the best value that still wears well
