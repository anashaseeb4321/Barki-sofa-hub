Upholstered Bed Sizes UK: Which Size Fits Your Room?

Single, small double, double, king or super king — the measurements, the floor space each one really needs, and how to choose without ending up with a room you can't walk around.

The short version

UK nameImperialMattress sizeBest for
Single3ft90 × 190 cmChildren's rooms, small box rooms
Small double4ft120 × 190 cmOne adult with room to spread out, tight guest rooms
Double4ft6135 × 190 cmThe UK standard for two adults
King5ft150 × 200 cmCouples who want real space; taller sleepers
Super king6ft180 × 200 cmLarge main bedrooms; co-sleeping families

Every one of our beds is made in all five sizes, and the mattress is included in the price — you are not quoted for a frame and then asked for the mattress separately.

One thing most size charts leave out. Those measurements are the mattress, not the bed. An upholstered frame adds padding and a headboard on top, so the space the bed actually occupies is larger. Message us with your model and size and we will send you the exact external measurements before you order.

How much floor space do you actually need?

The usual rule is to leave at least 60 cm of walking space on any side of the bed you need to get past, and around 75 cm in front of a wardrobe so the doors open properly.

Run through it before you choose:

  • Measure the wall the headboard will sit against, and the depth of the room from that wall
  • Subtract the mattress size, then check what is left against those clearances
  • Remember radiators, skirting boards and the swing of the bedroom door
  • If the room is under about 3 m wide, a 4ft6 double will usually sit better than a 5ft king

It is a common mistake to buy the largest bed a room will physically hold. A king bed in a room that only just takes it makes the room feel smaller than a double would, because you lose the floor space that makes a room feel open.

Small double or double?

The 4ft small double is the size people forget exists. At 120 cm it is 15 cm narrower than a standard double — enough to make a tight guest room work, and generous for one adult. Two adults sharing nightly will want the full 4ft6.

King or super king?

A 5ft king adds 15 cm of width over a double and, importantly, 10 cm of length — which matters if anyone in the bed is over about 6ft. The 6ft super king is 180 cm wide, which is a serious piece of furniture and needs a genuinely large room to look right rather than overwhelming.

Check your stairs and doorways too. A super king frame is wide, and upholstered beds do not fold.

Choosing the fabric

Bedroom upholstery takes far less punishment than a sofa, so you can choose on looks more freely than you could downstairs. A few practical notes:

  • Velvet gives the deepest colour and the most luxurious look, and it is the usual choice for a deep-buttoned headboard
  • Chenille is warmer to the touch and more forgiving if the bed gets leaned against daily
  • Light colours suit small rooms; deep teal, charcoal and navy work in larger rooms where the bed is meant to be the focal point
