Corner vs L-Shape vs U-Shape: Which Sofa Shape Fits Your Room?

These three names get used interchangeably by half the furniture trade, which makes comparing them harder than it should be. Here is what each one actually means, how much floor space each needs, and how to work out which suits your room.

The short version

ShapeTypical seatsRoom it suits
L-shape4 to 5Small and medium rooms; one wall plus a short return
Corner5 to 6Medium rooms with a genuine corner to fill
U-shape6 to 8Large rooms and open-plan spaces

If you only remember one thing: the shape should follow the room, not the other way round. A U-shape crammed into a small lounge does not seat more people comfortably, it just makes the room impossible to walk through.

What the names actually mean

The terms overlap, and different retailers use them differently, so it is worth being precise.

An L-shape is a sofa with a long main run and a shorter return at ninety degrees, usually in the form of a chaise. The return is typically shorter than the main body and often has no back on the end section.

A corner sofa is the same basic idea but with both arms closer in length and a proper corner seat where they meet. It reads as one continuous piece wrapping into the corner of a room, rather than a sofa with a chaise attached.

A U-shape has a main run with returns on both sides, enclosing three sides of a seating area. It is the largest of the three by some distance and needs the space to match.

In practice the line between L-shape and corner is blurry, and plenty of sellers use one word for both. What matters when you order is the actual measurements, not the label.

How much room each one needs

Forget the sofa's own dimensions for a moment. The number that decides whether a room works is the space left over. As a rough rule, leave at least 75cm of clear walkway between the sofa and anything opposite it, and around 40cm between the seat edge and a coffee table.

  • L-shape: works in rooms from about 3.5m × 3.5m upwards, because the short return keeps one side of the room open.
  • Corner: wants roughly 4m × 3.5m or more, and genuinely needs a corner — putting one in the middle of a long wall wastes what it is good at.
  • U-shape: realistically needs 4.5m × 4m or an open-plan area. Below that, you lose the room to the sofa.

These are guides, not rules. A made-to-order sofa can be built shorter or narrower than a standard one, which is often the difference between a shape working and not working. Our measuring guide walks through taking the numbers properly.

Which way should the corner face?

This catches more people out than any other decision, and it is the one that cannot be fixed afterwards.

Stand in the doorway and look at where the sofa will go. If the long arm runs to your right and the short return comes towards you on the right-hand side, that is a right-hand corner. If it runs to your left, it is a left-hand corner. Sellers should ask you this before building. If nobody asks, ask them.

Things that usually decide it for you: which wall has the radiator, where the television sits, where the door swings, and whether a bay window eats into one side.

Mark it out on the floor first. Masking tape on the carpet, in the exact footprint of the sofa you are considering, will tell you more in five minutes than any amount of measuring on paper. Walk around it. Open the door. Sit where the sofa will be.

Seating: the honest numbers

Seat counts in furniture advertising are optimistic. A “six seater” usually means six adults sitting shoulder to shoulder with nobody moving. For comfortable everyday use, take the advertised number and subtract one.

The corner seat itself is also worth thinking about. On a corner or U-shape, the seat in the actual corner is deep and slightly awkward — it tends to become the spot where people sit sideways with their legs up, rather than a normal seat. That is not a fault, it is just how corners work, but it means the usable seat count is often lower than the width suggests.

Getting it into the house

The bigger the shape, the more this matters. U-shapes and most corner sofas are made in sections precisely so they can be carried through ordinary doorways and up ordinary stairs. Before you order, measure the narrowest point on the route: the front door, the turn at the bottom of the stairs, the landing, and the door into the room itself.

Terraced and Victorian houses across Lancashire and Greater Manchester often have a tight turn at the foot of the stairs that is narrower than the front door. Tell your seller about it before the sofa is built, not on delivery day.

So which should you choose?

  • Small or awkward room: L-shape. The short return keeps floor space free.
  • You have a real corner to fill: corner sofa. It uses the space a rectangular sofa wastes.
  • Large family, big room, lots of guests: U-shape.
  • You move house often: L-shape or a 2+3 seater set — far easier to fit into the next place.
  • Open-plan room needing division: corner or U-shape, used to define the seating area.

Common questions

What is the difference between a corner sofa and an L-shape?

A corner sofa has arms of similar length meeting at a proper corner seat. An L-shape has a long main run with a shorter return, usually a chaise. The terms are often used interchangeably, so always check the actual measurements rather than the label.

How big a room do I need for a U-shape sofa?

Realistically about 4.5m by 4m, or an open-plan space. Below that you lose walkway room. A made-to-order U-shape can be built smaller than standard, which sometimes makes it work in a room that would otherwise be too tight.

How many people does a corner sofa seat?

It depends entirely on the length. A standard corner suite seats around five adults comfortably, though advertised seat counts tend to be optimistic. The corner seat itself is deep and usually ends up as a lounging spot rather than a normal seat.

Can I have the corner on the other side?

Yes, on a made-to-order sofa this costs nothing extra. Stand in your doorway: if the long arm runs to your right it is a right-hand corner, if to your left it is a left-hand corner. Decide before you order, because it cannot be changed afterwards.

Will a large sofa fit through my door?

Most corner and U-shape sofas are made in sections for exactly this reason. Measure the narrowest point on the route in — often the turn at the bottom of the stairs rather than the front door — and tell the seller before the sofa is made.
